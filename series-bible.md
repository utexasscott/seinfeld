## Season verdict — read this before ordering another one

**Larry flagged warmth in all five episodes. Five for five.** Ep 1: a priest catching
George called "the most decent act of the evening." Ep 2: Jerry sits with a corpse, then
gives away the raincoat. Ep 3: Jerry confesses alone to a closed door. Ep 4: Elaine nobly
declines the thing she wanted. Ep 5, the worst of them: *"you have found a way to catch
Jerry being a good man, and in the finale you built him a cathedral to do it in… the
funniest thing in the episode is a cake that says WELCOME BACK, and it's carrying the whole
hour on its back because everyone else is busy being moved."*

Every director read the warning, believed it had held the line, and drifted anyway — the
escalating capital-letter notes did not work. **A future season needs a structural fix, not
a louder note.** Candidates: forbid any scene where a character is alone and sincere; make
every act of generosity require an on-page invoice; hand the script doctor a veto instead
of a note.

**What did work:** the tag. Called out three episodes running, fixed in ep 4 (a pen on a
chain, about nothing), and never a problem again. A specific, mechanical instruction landed
where a thematic one didn't. **Write the notes that way.**

## Notes to the next director

- **Give them a want, not a punchline.** The cast names its own material — Vandelay, "the
  coward's K," "who am I Tuesday now," "a separate account." Set the want and get out.
- **Collide early.** Three people who each know something different, in one room, by the
  end of Act One. Every episode that did it paid immediately.
- **THE TAG IS NOT A RECAP — THREE IN A ROW AND LARRY HAS CALLED IT EVERY TIME.** Ep 1:
  Jerry explains the salad callback out loud. Ep 2: the tag explains the raincoat. Ep 3:
  *"the third tag in a row where he stands at a brick wall and tells the crowd what the
  episode was about."* Larry: **"If the story worked you don't need the summary, and if it
  didn't the summary won't save it."** Write a tag that is a new joke about an unrelated
  thing, or write no tag at all. Do not let Jerry name the theme, the callback, or the
  runner from the stage. If you catch yourself writing "which is exactly the —", stop.
- **WARMTH DRIFT — FLAGGED THREE EPISODES RUNNING. THE SEASON'S STANDING PROBLEM, AND IT
  IS GETTING WORSE, NOT BETTER.** Ep 3's verdict: *"You've now spent three straight
  episodes quietly making the man honest."* Jerry confessed the 81st Street snub
  **sincerely, unprompted, alone in a hallway, to a closed door, with nobody there to
  force it.** That is a man with a conscience. He does not have one. He lies until the
  lie costs more than the truth, and then he lies differently. **No character in this show
  volunteers a confession. Confessions are extracted, or they are performed for an
  audience the character is trying to manage.** Ep 1:
  a priest catching George, called "the most decent act of the evening." Ep 2, worse:
  Jerry sat with a dead man and said "neither of us has to be alone about it," then gave
  the raincoat away. Larry: *"Twice in one episode your lead character chose to be a good
  man, which is two more times than the format survives."* THE FORMAT HAS NO GROWTH AND
  NO KINDNESS. Nobody learns anything. Nobody gives anything away. If a character does
  something warm they must be buying something with it and the audience must see the price
  tag. **Every director so far believed it had held this line and none had — assume you
  are drifting warm right now.**
- **EP 4 DRIFTED AGAIN, THIS TIME ON ELAINE. FOUR FOR FOUR.** She turned down the front
  seat with *"I want you to say it someday when I'm not in the room. That's the version I
  want."* Larry: *"That's a woman who has learned the difference between winning and being
  loved — she's supposed to take the deal, get the four words, and discover they're
  worthless because Jerry said them; instead you gave her dignity and a lovely little ache,
  and the audience went 'aww.'"* **THE CORRECT SHAPE IS: THEY GET THE THING AND IT IS
  WORTHLESS.** Not: they nobly decline the thing. Declining is growth. Growth is the enemy.
  Whoever wants something should get it, on the worst possible terms, and be smaller for it.
- **The ep 4 tag was correct and is the model.** A pen on a chain, unrelated to anything,
  explaining nothing. Larry passed it without comment for the first time all season. **Do
  that again.**
- **A mute guest is a weapon.** Ray Kappel has never spoken. Sal Bass counted $91 out of a
  drawer without a word and destroyed Frank. Silence beats dialogue. Don't spawn everybody.
- **Length: ask for SHORT beats from turn one.** Ep 2 rolled 176 and landed at 81 because
  every reply came back as a four-paragraph aria. Ep 3 held short-beat discipline from the
  first ping and landed 113 against a 113–153 window with much tighter volleys. Keep it.
  Batch three characters at a time on independent beats, then order them on the page.
- **Ep 4's own failure: Act One ran thin — thirteen turns to launch four plots.** Jerry's
  car plot was *asserted*, never dramatized; the audience never saw him want the car for
  anything. **Give every character a concrete errand in Act One, not a position.**
- **Ep 3's own failure: two subplots went under-serviced.** George's Trotta thread
  resolved in one silent door-open and never paid off, and **Jerry's girlfriend Valerie
  never appeared in a single scene** — his A-plot romance was an offstage rumor. **If you
  build a plot on a relationship, put the person in a room.** Also: over-batching in Act
  Two put a few reactions a beat out of order. **Check chronology before you append, not
  after.**
