# Episode 5 — The Urn

**DRAW:** an urn on a mantel / an all-night pharmacy with one register open · a hardware store where nobody works in the aisle you need · a rent-controlled apartment being shown to strangers · a U-Haul return lot fifteen minutes after closing / not tipping and being seen not tipping · splitting a check to your own advantage / who owns a story that happened to two people · whether a gift becomes yours the moment you touch it · whether an apology delivered to the wrong person still counts / Uncle Leo, who traps someone and will not release them

*(Deck replenished before the roll — every burned venue, object, transgression and rule replaced in place. These are live cards.)*

**THEME:** What becomes yours the second nobody's left to claim it.

**A-PLOT (Jerry):** Asked to eulogize a man he never met, because a story he's been telling all over town got attached to the wrong corpse. The urn is at the all-night pharmacy. There is one register and a tip jar.
**B-PLOT (Kramer):** Three hundred and twelve dollars collected from the building for a memorial. A forty-dollar urn. A cake box tied with string. And a mantel that has to be bolted to somebody else's wall.
**C-PLOT (George):** A quart of milk with a dead man's name written on it in marker, drunk from the carton in front of eleven people. He will apologize. He will apologize to the wrong man.
**D-PLOT (Elaine):** 5F. Rent-controlled, three-forty, shown to strangers Wednesday. All she has to do is prove she was somebody to him.

**COLLISION:** The memorial is in Jerry's apartment, because 5F is being shown at the same hour. Everyone in the room is claiming a piece of a man none of them knew. Newman has the receipt on all of it and a price. Bevilacqua is not dead.

But I've only got four minutes, and there's a gentleman in row three in a raincoat who knew the deceased longer than anybody in this apartment — and who was also, I'm told, on Eighty-first Street around that time.

    (a small open hand toward row three)
Sir. Take my four minutes. Tell them how it went.

I'll be at the cake.

He goes to the cake and cuts himself a piece with the flower on it.

(BIG LAUGH)

Twenty heads turn to row three.

RAY KAPPEL stands up.

He looks at Jerry.

He does not say anything.

He does not say anything for a very long time.

Somewhere in row six, a woman starts to cry.

Ray Kappel sits back down.

(BIG LAUGH)

JERRY
And *that* — that's what I was talking about. You see it? He got up, he gave us nothing, and row six is in tears. Nothing! The man is a genius. I do thirty-five minutes and I don't get that.

That's the building's tribute right there, ladies and gentlemen. Beautiful. Just beautiful. Nobody follow that.

There's cake. There's a half sheet. Somebody take the flower piece away from Newman, he's already had it.

(BIG LAUGH)

Chairs scrape. Coats come off the bed. ELAINE cuts across the room to Kramer and puts two fingers on his clipboard.

ELAINE
Kramer. Clipboard. Now, while they're still in their coats — you heard the ruling, you *made* the ruling, I'm the one who was held.

Write it. B-e-n-e-s. Print it, don't do your loops, nobody can read your loops.

Showing's at ten Wednesday. If that name isn't up there by tomorrow morning I will come to your apartment and I will take the clipboard.

(BIG LAUGH)

Kramer clicks the pen. Squares the clipboard. One long breath, like a man signing a treaty.

KRAMER
B... e... n... e... s. Printed. No loops.

There. That's up. That's official, that's the chair's hand, nobody can take that off you.

    (a small hitch — he looks at the door, then back at her)
Just — don't go measuring anything, Elaine. Not this week. A thing like this, you gotta let it *settle*.

(LAUGHTER)

SMASH CUT TO:

EXT. U-HAUL RETURN LOT, WEST SIDE — 9:15 PM

A chain-link gate, chained. Behind it, sixty trucks and one lit window with the back of a man's head in it. Bolted to the fence, a steel drop box under a sign:

**AFTER-HOURS RETURN — $50 SERVICE FEE + ONE FULL ADDITIONAL DAY**

GEORGE, alone, key in his fist.

GEORGE
Fifteen minutes. FIFTEEN. There's a man in there, I can see the light, I can see the back of his HEAD —

Fifty dollars and a full additional day. So the eighty's gone. The eighty is gone and I still have the credenza. I'm renting a truck to store a credenza, that's — that's a storage unit with a steering wheel!

I carried it down five flights on my own. Five. I have a story for that room and nobody wanted it, and Leo takes the arm, and Kramer's got a GAVEL —

You know what? Fine. I'm keeping it another day. I'm a great quitter but I don't quit at NINE FIFTEEN.

He slaps the drop box and walks back to the truck.

(BIG LAUGH)

SMASH CUT TO:

INT. JERRY'S APARTMENT — 9:40 PM

Chairs stacked. Cake a wreck. The urn still on the shelf. JERRY, KRAMER, ELAINE and NEWMAN are the only ones left standing. UNCLE LEO is asleep in a folding chair with a paper plate on his chest.

The door opens. GEORGE.

GEORGE
The lot was chained. So I'm into a full additional day and a fifty-dollar service fee, and there's a credenza double-parked on Eighty-First Street.

I got it down alone. I can't get it up alone. That's — that's the whole speech, that's all I came in here with.

Somebody take an end. I'm asking. I'm not trading, I'm not offering anything, I'm ASKING.

(LAUGHTER)

JERRY
You took a credenza out of a dead man's apartment, and it's double-parked on *Eighty-first Street*. Of all the streets in Manhattan. That's where you left it.

I'm not carrying furniture at nine forty at night. I've got a hole in my wall, an urn on a shelf, and my uncle asleep in a chair with a plate on him. I'm at capacity.

Talk to Newman. He's had two pieces of cake and four minutes of stage time tonight — he owes somebody something.

(BIG LAUGH)

Newman sets down his plate. He has been waiting all night for somebody to need something.

NEWMAN
A credenza. Solid oak, dead man's credenza, four flights, no elevator after nine. Oh, I'll carry it, George. I'll carry it like it was my own mother.

Here's who pays. Kramer's two hundred and seventy-one comes off the top — that's the management fee, and I'm the management now. Elaine covers the fifty-dollar U-Haul, because that credenza is going into 5F and 5F is her name on the clipboard.

And Jerry. You take the low end and you walk backwards down the stairs, and we're square on Eighty-first Street. Tonight only.

(BIG LAUGH)

ELAINE
You took a credenza. Out of *my* apartment. My name is printed on the clipboard, George, that credenza is a fixture, that's my credenza.

Our deal was for a dead man's furniture. It is now a live woman's furniture and the deal is void.

Bring it back up. Bring it back up tonight, and you're going to want the dolly, because I'm standing right here watching you do the stairs.

(BIG LAUGH)

Something goes in George. It is the sound of a man reaching for the last card in the deck.

GEORGE
A FIXTURE? A credenza is not a fixture! A fixture is BOLTED! I know what a fixture is — I'm an ARCHITECT!

I did the bandshell. The Parks Department bandshell, that's mine, that's my elevation, people sit on the grass in front of my WORK on a Sunday —

So I'm not gonna stand here and get zoned by a clipboard and a mailman. I have credentials. I have a stamp!

And nobody in this room has ever asked me one question about it. Not ONE. You ever wonder why that is? Huh? HUH?

(BIG LAUGH)

In the folding chair by the wall, the paper plate slides off Uncle Leo's chest and hits the floor.

He is awake.

LEO
THE BANDSHELL? YOU did the bandshell? Sweetheart — YOU'RE the short nervous fellow from the elevator! You have a stamp, you have an elevation, people sit on the GRASS in front of your work on a Sunday and nobody in this room asks you one question!

My son JEFFREY. Parks Department, eleven years, he knows every project in the five boroughs. I'm gonna have Jeffrey look into it. I'm gonna have Jeffrey look into it TONIGHT.

Sit down, we're gonna go through this bandshell start to finish.

He takes George by the left arm, above the elbow, and does not let go.

(BIG LAUGH)

GEORGE
Jeffrey doesn't have to look into anything. Tell Jeffrey it's fine. Tell Jeffrey it's a — it's under a different filing name, it's a whole thing, he'd be wasting a call.

Leo. Leo, the arm. The ARM, Leo, that's the same arm, that's the same spot, above the elbow —

I never said it was ME in the elevator. Somebody else said that. I sold a STORY, I didn't sell a — LET GO OF THE ARM!

(BIG LAUGH)

Elaine has not moved. She is looking at the clipboard on the counter.

ELAINE
Yeah. It was George's. I bought it, eighty dollars, cash, there's no receipt and I'm not embarrassed.

The room's empty, fellas. Twenty people went home moved, and my name is printed on that clipboard in the chair's own hand. That's the part that's in ink.

You want to be right about this at ten o'clock Wednesday? Be right. I'll be inside.

(BIG LAUGH)

And the door opens.

A man is standing in it. Seventy-odd. A tan you can only get in Florida. A suitcase in one hand, a folded raincoat over his arm, and his keys already out.

He looks at the twenty folding chairs. He looks at the wrecked half sheet on the counter that says WELCOME BACK. He looks at the forty-one dollar urn bolted to the wall at eye height.

Nobody in the room says anything at all.

Except Kramer, who is the only person present who is not surprised.

KRAMER
    (arms wide, delighted, crossing to him without a flicker)
*Bevilacqua!* Tuesday! I said Tuesday, didn't I say Tuesday — look at that color on him, people!

    (turning the wrecked cake around so the room can read it)
Welcome back. It says it right on the cake. Everybody — chairs front — he's *home*.

(BIG LAUGH)

JERRY
The cake. The cake was *right*. The cake knew.

Kramer. Look at your face. You're the only person in this room who isn't surprised, and I want everybody to notice that.

Mr. Bevilacqua — welcome home. Whatever you heard from the hallway in the last three hours, I'd like it on the record that I was *handed* this.

(BIG LAUGH)

Elaine is across the room before Jerry finishes the sentence.

ELAINE
Mr. Bevilacqua. Hi. You look wonderful, you look — tan. Great.

Listen, before anybody says anything. Are you *keeping* the apartment? Because the committee's already gone to paper on this, there's a process, there's a name up, and it would be a real shame to waste the whole evening.

We had a beautiful night for you. Twenty people. I stood up. So — think about Florida. Think hard about Florida.

(BIG LAUGH)

Newman sets down his plate for the second time tonight. He is having the finest evening of his life.

NEWMAN
Look at that tan.

Kramer. You didn't blink. Twenty people jumped out of their skin and you didn't blink. You knew Tuesday morning and you passed a coffee can anyway — that's not a management fee, buddy, that's the mail fraud statute, and I know it by heart, I have a poster of it.

Which leaves one question for the room, and I want everybody quiet for it.

Whose ashes are on Jerry's wall?

He laughs. Low, and long.

(BIG LAUGH)

Every head in the room turns to the shelf.

KRAMER
Nobody's! It came empty, Newman — it's an urn, they ship 'em empty, that's the whole business.

But an empty urn on a shelf, that's — that's *sad*, so I put something in it so it wouldn't rattle.

    (a look dead at George)
That letter to the Community Board. The one somebody wrote and signed my name to. I burned it and I funneled it. And I feel *terrific*.

(BIG LAUGH)

George, still held by the arm, does not defend himself on the letter. He has bigger business.

GEORGE
It counts. It COUNTS. I gave it at full strength, on my knees, in front of twenty people — that's not a rehearsal, that's a PERFORMANCE, and it happened.

Who it landed on is a clerical issue. That's routing. You don't make a man apologize twice because the ROOM sat him in the wrong chair!

I get one. That was it. That was mine and I spent it and I'm not doing it again for the milk, for the furniture, or for you, sir, tanned as you are.

(BIG LAUGH)

Jerry has not taken his eyes off the shelf.

JERRY
You burned it. The only copy. And you funneled it into a forty-one dollar urn and bolted it to my wall at eye height.

So that's it. That letter is gone, and whoever wrote it is never going to be found, and now it's a *decoration*. In my apartment. Forever.

    (a hand on George's shoulder, warm, generous)
George. Buddy. Let it go. It's over. You'll never have to talk about it again as long as you live.

And through all of it, quietly, Mr. Bevilacqua has set down his suitcase.

He picks up his suitcase and goes to the door.

On his way out he passes row three, where RAY KAPPEL — who has not left, and has not spoken, in three hours — stands up and puts out his hand.

Bevilacqua walks straight past him and out the door.

Ray Kappel stands there with his hand out.

He puts it down.

He sits back down.

Jerry has watched every second of it. So has nobody else.

## Note

"He's not being rude. He's being honest." The room goes still, row six cries, and then you close on Ray Kappel with his hand out and the line "Jerry has watched every second of it. So has nobody else." That's a lovely short film and it is not this show — five for five now, you have found a way to catch Jerry being a good man, and in the finale you built him a cathedral to do it in. The funniest thing in the episode is a cake that says WELCOME BACK, and it's carrying the whole hour on its back because everyone else is busy being moved.
